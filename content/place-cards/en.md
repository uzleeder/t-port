## Humo Arena
Tashkent · Sports & events

**History & significance.** Opened in March 2019, Humo Arena gave Tashkent a major venue for ice hockey, figure skating and large public events. Its opening marked an investment in Uzbekistan’s winter sports.

**What to see.** Look for the arena’s sweeping silhouette. Depending on the programme, you may find a match, competition or stage show.

**Plan your visit.** Check the organiser’s event calendar and ticket information before you go.

Sources

- [www.president.uz](https://www.president.uz/ru/lists/view/2433)

## Hazrati Imam
Tashkent · Old City · Religious architecture

**History & significance.** This ensemble grew around the tomb of Abu Bakr Qaffal Shashi, a scholar associated with historic Tashkent. Madrasas, a mausoleum and a mosque represent different periods of the city’s religious life.

**What to see.** Look for the Barak-Khan Madrasa portal, Qaffal Shashi’s mausoleum and the Muyi Muborak Madrasa.

**Plan your visit.** Dress respectfully and check visitor access around prayer times.

Sources

- [visit.tashkent.uz](https://visit.tashkent.uz/uz/popular-route/)

## Charvak Dam
Tashkent Region · Bostanlyk · Engineering & landscape

**History & significance.** Built near the confluence of the Chatkal and Pskem rivers, the dam created a reservoir that forms part of the Chirchiq basin’s water and power infrastructure.

**What to see.** From an open viewpoint, take in the reservoir, the dam and the slopes of the western Tian Shan in a single panorama.

**Plan your visit.** Use designated public viewpoints and check road conditions before setting out.

## Amirsoy
Tashkent Region · Bostanlyk · Mountain resort

**History & significance.** Opened in 2019 near Chimgan, Amirsoy serves winter skiers and snowboarders with groomed slopes and gondola, chair and conveyor lifts.

**What to see.** Ski or snowboard in winter, or take the gondola for mountain views when the lifts are operating outside ski season.

**Plan your visit.** Check the resort’s lift and slope status alongside the weather forecast.

## Registan Square
Samarkand · Historic square

**History & significance.** Three madrasas frame the square: Ulugh Beg, Sher-Dor and Tilla-Kari. Ulugh Beg’s madrasa dates to the 15th century. The other two were added later.

**What to see.** Compare Ulugh Beg’s geometric patterns, the animals on Sher-Dor’s portal and Tilla-Kari’s gilded interior.

**Plan your visit.** Start in the square to see all three façades, then take time to explore their courtyards and details.

Sources

- [whc.unesco.org](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/603)
- [travmap.org](https://travmap.org/route/1934)

## Shah-i-Zinda
Samarkand · Mausoleum complex

**History & significance.** Set against the Afrasiab hillside, Shah-i-Zinda is a stepped avenue of mausoleums and one of the principal monuments in UNESCO-listed historic Samarkand.

**What to see.** Follow the succession of portals and look closely at the blue tilework, glazed relief and inscriptions.

**Plan your visit.** Wear shoes suited to steps and give worshippers space when taking photographs.

Sources

- [whc.unesco.org](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/603)

## Poi Kalon
Bukhara · Old City · Architectural ensemble

**History & significance.** The ensemble brings together the Kalon Minaret, Kalon Mosque and Mir-i Arab Madrasa. The minaret was built in 1127 during the reign of the Qarakhanid ruler Arslan Khan.

**What to see.** Notice the patterned brick bands on the minaret and how the mosque and madrasa portals face one another across the square.

**Plan your visit.** Compare the façades from the square and check access rules before entering religious buildings.

Sources

## Lyabi-Hauz
Bukhara · Old City · Historic pool

**History & significance.** Lyabi-Hauz takes its name from the pool at the heart of the ensemble. Nearby buildings include the Nadir Divan-Begi Madrasa and khanqah, and the Kukeldash Madrasa.

**What to see.** Walk the shaded perimeter and look for the Nadir Divan-Begi portal. The pool and surrounding buildings show how public life and trade converged in old Bukhara.

**Plan your visit.** Include it in an Old City walking route and pause at different sides of the pool.

Sources

- [uzbekistan.travel](https://uzbekistan.travel/en/o/lyabi-hauz-ensemble/)

## Kalta Minor
Khiva · Khorezm · Unfinished minaret

**History & significance.** Muhammad Amin Khan commissioned this minaret beside his madrasa. Construction stopped in 1855 after his death, leaving Khiva with its distinctive broad, low tower.

**What to see.** Look for the turquoise glazed tiles and compare their pattern with the decoration on the neighbouring madrasa portal.

**Plan your visit.** Enter the walled city via the West Gate and visit Kalta Minor on foot.

Sources

- [uzbekistan.travel](https://uzbekistan.travel/en/o/kalta-minor-khiva/)
- [whc.unesco.org](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/543)

## Itchan Kala
Khiva · Khorezm · Walled inner city

**History & significance.** Khiva’s inner town was a caravan stop before the desert crossing toward Persia. UNESCO added the walled city to the World Heritage List in 1990.

**What to see.** See the West Gate, Kunya Ark, the wooden columns of the Juma Mosque and the courtyards of Tash Hauli Palace.

**Plan your visit.** Explore on foot and confirm which museums or courtyards require tickets at the entrance.

Sources

- [whc.unesco.org](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/543)

## Khudayor Khan Palace
Kokand · Fergana Region · Palace architecture

**History & significance.** Commissioned by Khudayor Khan, the palace reflects the court architecture of the Kokand Khanate. Uzbekistan’s tourism portal identifies it as the ruler’s seventh palace.

**What to see.** Look for the colourful tiled entrance, decorated doors and windows, and the courtyards behind the main portal.

**Plan your visit.** Pair the palace with Kokand’s Jami Mosque and a walk through the old town.

Sources

- [uzbekistan.travel](https://uzbekistan.travel/en/o/khudoyar-khan-palace/)

## Rishtan Ceramics
Rishtan · Fergana Region · Traditional crafts

**History & significance.** Rishtan is one of the Fergana Valley’s pottery centres. Local workshops offer a view of shaping, decorating, glazing and firing. Uzbekistan’s ceramic arts entered UNESCO’s intangible heritage list in 2023.

**What to see.** Compare the blue and green patterns on bowls and platters, and look at how different motifs are painted.

**Plan your visit.** Arrange a workshop visit in advance and ask a potter to explain the glaze and firing process.

Sources

- [ich.unesco.org](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ceramic-arts-in-uzbekistan-01989)
- [toureast.uz](https://toureast.uz/en/blog/fergana-valley-3-day-itinerary)

## Babur Memorial Park
Bogishamol · Andijan Region · Memorial park

**History & significance.** On Bogishamol Hill, the park commemorates Zahir al-Din Muhammad Babur, who was born in Andijan. Soil was brought from Agra, where he died, and Kabul, where he was buried.

**What to see.** See the memorial and hilltop views, then combine the visit with the museum devoted to Babur.

**Plan your visit.** Plan your transport back to central Andijan and allow time for a walk around the hill.

Sources

- [app.uzbekistan.travel](https://app.uzbekistan.travel/en/o/babur-memorial-park-and-house-museum/)

Chartak · Namangan Region · Valley retreat

**History & significance.** In the Chartaksoy Valley, Chartak is known for its mineral water and sanatoriums. Its foothill setting puts orchards and valley scenery within reach of Namangan.

## Ak-Saray Palace Ruins
Shahrisabz · Kashkadarya · Timurid palace

**History & significance.** Built under Amir Timur, Ak-Saray survives mainly in the two towering sides of its entrance portal. It is a principal monument of the historic centre of Shahrisabz.

**What to see.** Study the surviving tile patterns and stand back to appreciate the scale of the original entrance.

**Plan your visit.** View the portal from across the approach, then continue on foot to Dorut Tilovat.

Sources

## Sultan Saodat
Termez · Surkhandarya · Mausoleum complex

**History & significance.** The burial place of the Sayyids of Termez took shape over several centuries. Its surviving mausoleums reflect different building phases through the 17th century.

**What to see.** Follow the courtyard to study brick portals, the iwan and the placement of the mausoleums. Fired brick is a defining material here.

**Plan your visit.** Combine it with the Termez Archaeological Museum for a broader view of the area’s history.

Sources

- [uzbekistan.travel](https://uzbekistan.travel/en/o/sultan-saodat/)

## Boysun
Boysun District · Surkhandarya · Mountains & living heritage

**History & significance.** Boysun’s mountain communities have preserved local songs, ceremonies and crafts across generations. UNESCO recognises the cultural space of Boysun District as intangible heritage.

**What to see.** A local guide can help you explore village life and mountain routes with context about the customs you encounter.

**Plan your visit.** Check road and trail conditions. Ask permission before photographing people or ceremonies.

## Yogochli Sardoba
Gumbaz Village · Syrdarya Region · Historic water cistern

**History & significance.** This cistern on an old caravan route across the Mirzachul steppe collected and stored water. Its fired-brick dome sheltered the water supply in an arid landscape.

**What to see.** Notice the dome, entrance and how the structure sits against the surrounding ground.

**Plan your visit.** Confirm access locally. If the interior is closed, you can still study the building from outside.

## Syr Darya Riverbank
Syrdarya Region · River landscape

**History & significance.** The Syr Darya is one of Central Asia’s major rivers and historically one of the Aral Sea’s two main feeder rivers. In this region it meets the open Mirzachul plain.

**What to see.** Look for reed beds, open water and broad horizons from accessible public stretches of the riverbank.

**Plan your visit.** Choose a specific public viewpoint before leaving. This card represents the wider river landscape.

## Nurata Chashma
Nurata · Navoi Region · Sacred spring

**History & significance.** The Chashma complex developed around a spring in Nurata. The ruins of Nur Fortress on the hill above it are traditionally associated with Alexander the Great.

**What to see.** See the spring pool, domed buildings and fortress remains. The hill opens onto views of Nurata and the steppe beyond.

**Plan your visit.** Respect local pilgrimage customs and wear shoes suited to uneven ground if climbing toward the fortress.

## Savitsky Museum
Nukus · Karakalpakstan · Art museum

**History & significance.** Founded in 1966, the I. V. Savitsky State Museum of Art brings together Karakalpak applied arts and major examples of 20th-century avant-garde art.

**What to see.** Alongside the paintings, make time for textiles, jewellery and other examples of local craftsmanship.

**Plan your visit.** Check the museum’s official information for galleries that are open and exhibitions on view.

## Moynaq Ship Graveyard
Moynaq · Karakalpakstan · Open-air museum

**History & significance.** Fishing vessels sit on dry ground near the former Aral Sea port. They tell the story of a fishing town whose shoreline retreated as the sea shrank.

**What to see.** Examine the exposed hulls and former seabed, then visit the museum for more context on the human and environmental history.

**Plan your visit.** Plan a separate excursion from Nukus and bring water and sun protection for the exposed site.

Sources

- [karakalpakstan.travel](https://karakalpakstan.travel/museums-and-exhibitions/ship-graveyard/)
